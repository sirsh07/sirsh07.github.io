How this explorer should guide the proposal

Develop

Use harmonized NHANES 2003–2018 for model development, with cycle-aware resampling and complex-survey methods where population inference is intended.

Validate

Freeze the model before using NHANES 2021–2023 for temporal validation and the Florida serum cohort for true external validation and recalibration.

Contextualize

Use EPA, USGS, and Florida water data to characterize geography and exposure domains. Do not treat water measurements as substitutes for person-level serum PFAS.

Interpretation: released file rows are not final analytic sample sizes. Age restrictions, missing outcomes, covariates, assay detection, fasting requirements, and repeated observations all affect usable n.

Individual studies and cohorts

NHANES

Lin et al., 2010

n=2,216 adults · U.S. · NHANES 1999–2000 and 2003–2004.

PFOA was positively associated with ALT and GGT; some effects were stronger with obesity, insulin resistance, or metabolic syndrome.

Data: NHANES is open, but this exact analytic file is not separately released.

Primary paper ↗
NHANES

Gleason et al., 2015

n=4,333 participants · U.S. · NHANES 2007–2010.

Compound-specific associations were reported across ALT, AST, GGT, ALP and bilirubin; PFOA showed positive ALT/GGT/bilirubin signals.

Data: reconstructable from open NHANES.

Primary paper ↗
NHANES

Jain & Ducatman, 2019

n=2,883 adults across obese and non-obese strata · NHANES 2011–2014.

PFOA, PFHxS and PFNA were associated with higher ALT, and PFOA/PFNA with GGT, primarily among participants with obesity.

Data: reconstructable from open NHANES.

Primary paper ↗
Histology

Jin et al., 2020

n=74 children with diagnosed NAFLD · Atlanta · 2007–2015.

Per-IQR PFOS and PFHxS were associated with NASH; PFHxS was associated with fibrosis (OR 4.44), lobular inflammation, and higher activity score.

Data: pediatric liver-biopsy repository; no direct public person-level download.

Open paper ↗
Prospective

Goodrich et al., 2022

50 incident non-viral HCC cases + 50 controls nested in the Multiethnic Cohort.

High pre-diagnostic PFOS was associated with higher hepatocellular carcinoma risk, with metabolomic signals involving glucose, amino-acid and bile-acid pathways.

Data: controlled cohort; small proof-of-concept analysis.

Open paper ↗
Exposed community

Steenland et al., 2009

n=46,294 adults · Mid-Ohio Valley C8 Health Project · 2005–2006.

Total cholesterol rose about 11–12 mg/dL from the lowest to highest PFOA or PFOS decile; most lipid outcomes increased, but HDL did not.

Data: no direct public person-level download.

Primary paper ↗
Children

Frisbee et al., 2010

12,476 children and adolescents · C8 Health Project.

PFOA and PFOS showed positive, nonlinear associations with total and LDL cholesterol; increases were larger at the lower exposure range.

Data: no direct public person-level download.

Open paper ↗
NHANES

Nelson et al., 2010

n=860 adults for TC/HDL/non-HDL; n=416 for LDL · NHANES 2003–2004.

Highest versus lowest quartiles corresponded to +13.4 mg/dL TC for PFOS, +9.8 for PFOA, and +13.9 for PFNA; PFHxS was inverse.

Data: reconstructable from open NHANES.

Open paper ↗
Longitudinal

Fitz-Simon et al., 2013

n=560 C8 follow-up participants without lipid-lowering therapy; LDL analysis n=521.

Declines in PFOA and PFOS over about four years were associated with declines in total and LDL cholesterol, strengthening temporal evidence.

Data: cohort-controlled, not a direct public download.

Open paper ↗
Longitudinal

PIVUS study, 2022

n=864 at age 70; 614 and 404 re-examined at ages 75 and 80 · Sweden.

Changes in six of eight PFAS were positively associated with changes in plasma lipids across three measurements over ten years.

Data: request-based cohort.

Primary paper ↗
Trial cohort

DPP Outcomes Study, 2019

n=888 pre-diabetic adults without lipid-lowering medication at baseline.

Baseline PFAS were related to several lipid fractions, with longitudinal analyses testing whether lifestyle intervention modified trajectories.

Data: DPP controlled access; not an anonymous public file.

Open paper ↗
Exposed community

GenX lipid study, 2022

n=326 · Wilmington, NC · 2017–2018.

Per-quartile PFOS and PFNA increases corresponded to roughly 5 mg/dL higher non-HDL and 6 mg/dL higher total cholesterol.

Data: investigator request; related map cohort.

Reviews, meta-analyses, and evidence syntheses

Meta-analysis

Costello et al., 2022

24 epidemiologic + 85 rodent studies.

Human evidence linked ALT with PFOA, PFOS and PFNA; PFOA was also linked with AST/GGT. Animal evidence consistently supported steatosis and liver injury.
Open paper ↗
Meta-analysis

Liu et al., 2023

29 adult epidemiological studies.

PFOA and PFOS were positively associated with selected blood lipids, especially total cholesterol and LDL; heterogeneity was substantial.
Open paper ↗
Review

Fenton et al., 2021

Epidemiologic review of PFAS with obesity, diabetes and NAFLD.

Animal evidence for hepatic lipid disruption was stronger than the still-limited human evidence for clinically defined NAFLD/NASH.
Open paper ↗
Toxicological profile

ATSDR, 2021

Integrated human and experimental evidence for multiple PFAS health outcomes.

Summarizes recurring liver-enzyme and lipid findings while emphasizing compound-, population-, and design-specific uncertainty.
Official profile ↗
New synthesis

Updated dyslipidemia review, 2025

69 articles reviewed; 37 meta-analyzed.

Pooled estimates supported positive PFOA/PFOS associations with total and LDL cholesterol, but the authors judged causal certainty insufficient.

Prospective pooled analysis

12-cohort liver-cancer analysis, 2025

Individual-participant nested case-control analysis across 12 prospective cohorts.

The larger pooled evidence did not establish a clear overall PFAS–liver-cancer association, tempering the earlier 50-case HCC signal.

Evidence-to-proposal flowchart

1. PFAS exposure

Serum PFOA, PFOS, PFHxS, PFNA and PFDA are repeatedly detected in population biomonitoring; exposed communities can have different mixtures and concentrations.

NHANES · C8 · GenX

→

2. Metabolic response

Human and experimental evidence implicates hepatic lipid, bile-acid, amino-acid and glucose pathways; mixture and nonlinear effects are plausible.

Costello · Goodrich · Yan

→

3. Observable outcomes

The clearest recurring human signals are liver enzymes and total/LDL/non-HDL cholesterol. Steatosis, fibrosis, HDL, triglycerides and cancer are less consistent.

Borghese · Liu · Momo

Most important conclusion

The literature already establishes that PFAS–liver and PFAS–lipid associations can be modeled in NHANES. The research gap is whether those nationally learned relationships remain calibrated in a specifically exposed Florida population with a different mixture, exposure distribution, recruitment process, and clinical context.

Primary outcomes: ALT, AST, GGT, ALP, total cholesterol, LDL and non-HDL. Secondary outcomes: bilirubin, HDL, triglycerides and FIB-4. Exploratory: steatosis/MASLD definitions, fibrosis, subgroup-specific nonlinearities, and emerging PFAS.

Required design: develop in harmonized NHANES → lock the model → validate temporally in 2021–2023 → externally validate and recalibrate in Florida → use UCMR/Florida water data only as environmental context. Association and prediction should not be described as proof of causation.
