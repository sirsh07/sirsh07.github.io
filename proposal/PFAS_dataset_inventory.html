Bottom line

The papers did not train one shared machine-learning model. Each fitted statistical models to its own cohort. For your proposed national-to-local study, NHANES is the only immediately downloadable, person-level human source that jointly supplies serum PFAS, liver markers, lipids, covariates, and survey design variables at useful scale.

NHANES: use nowOpen person-level U.S. data suitable for primary development, internal validation, trends, and temporal validation.

PFAS harmonization that matters

  • Safest cross-cycle core: PFOA, PFOS, PFHxS, PFNA, PFDA/PFDeA, and often PFUnDA/PFUA. Detection prevalence should be checked before final inclusion.
  • 2013 onward: PFOA and PFOS may be split into linear and branched isomers. Define total PFOA and total PFOS consistently, and preserve isomer-specific variables for sensitivity analyses.
  • Below detection: use the released value/comment-code convention appropriate to each cycle; do not silently apply one rule to every period.
  • Weights: build multi-cycle subsample weights and retain strata/PSU variables. Unweighted prediction can answer a different question, but population inference requires the complex design.

Official sources: NHANES laboratory file search; 1999–2000 PFAS documentation; 2013–14 core file; 2013–14 isomer file; 2017–March 2020 file; 2021–23 file. Counts were checked directly against the released XPT files.

Recommended dataset architecture

Develop and internally validate in harmonized NHANES 2003–2018
→
Freeze model; test out of time in NHANES 2021–2023
→
Externally validate, recalibrate, and adapt in the Florida cohort

Best defensible plan

  1. Primary development: NHANES 2003–2018, with prespecified harmonization of PFAS isomers, laboratory methods, LLODs, outcomes and multi-cycle weights. Use cycle-aware resampling—not a random split that leaks time-period information.
  2. Temporal validation: hold out 2021–2023 entirely until model choices are frozen. Report calibration as well as discrimination/error.
  3. True external validation: use the Florida serum cohort. Lock the intersecting PFAS, outcome definitions, units and covariates before looking at performance.
  4. Contextual exposure layer: link UCMR 5 and Florida DEP water-system information where geography and dates permit. Keep environmental and serum exposures conceptually separate.
  5. Optional replication: pursue CHMS or GenX access only if the timeline supports approvals; do not make proposal success depend on unapproved data.

What to request from the Florida cohort before analysis

  • Exact participant n, recruitment years, counties/water systems, inclusion/exclusion criteria, repeated visits, and specimen timing.
  • PFAS analyte list, units, matrix, assay method, LOD/LLOQ, detection frequencies, isomer reporting, batch variables and storage conditions.
  • ALT, AST, GGT, ALP, total bilirubin, platelet count, total cholesterol, HDL, LDL, triglycerides, fasting status, medication use and laboratory reference ranges.
  • Core adjustment variables aligned with NHANES: age, sex, race/ethnicity, BMI, smoking, alcohol, diabetes, kidney function, socioeconomic measures and survey/recruitment structure.
  • A data dictionary plus a blinded feasibility count for complete exposure–outcome–covariate overlap. This number, not total enrollment, determines external-validation power.
Practical conclusion: begin coding with NHANES immediately. Treat Florida as the required external test set, not as extra rows to mix into model training. Use water datasets to explain exposure context and potential domain shift, not as substitutes for serum measurements.
